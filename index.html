Buying Direct

How a For Sale by Owner
purchase works.

Buying a home directly from the owner is straightforward in Texas. No listing agent means a more direct conversation about price and terms — and savings that show up in the asking. Here's the path:

01

Schedule a viewing

Call or text (210) 651-2607 to arrange a private showing at a time that works for you. Drive-by viewing of the exterior is welcome anytime — interior tours are by appointment so we can give you the full walkthrough.

Bring questions. The owner knows every quirk of the house and will answer anything.

02

Submit a written offer

Texas uses the TREC One to Four Family Residential Contract (form 20-17). Your buyer's agent, real estate attorney, or even a transaction coordinator can prepare it. The forms are also free on the TREC website.

Include a mortgage pre-approval letter or proof of funds with your offer. It's not legally required but it dramatically strengthens your position.

03

Close at a title company

Once terms are agreed, earnest money goes to a title company (we'll pick one together — Hays-area title companies are familiar with FSBO closings). The title company handles escrow, title search, deed prep, and the closing disclosure.

Standard Texas closing timeline is 30–45 days from contract to keys. Faster for cash offers.

Do I need a buyer's agent?

No. Many buyers use one anyway for the contract-and-negotiation expertise — and that's fine. The seller is not paying buyer's agent commission, so this is something you'd arrange and pay separately. Plenty of buyers also do this themselves with a real estate attorney's help (typically a few hundred dollars for a contract review). Either way works.

BUYING DIRECT (FSBO PROCESS)
Do I need a real estate agent to buy this home? +

No. This is a For Sale by Owner listing. Buyers can either:

  • Bring their own buyer's agent — the seller is not paying buyer's agent commission, so any agent fees would be negotiated between you and your agent. Many buyer's agents are willing to work this out.
  • Represent themselves — using the standard TREC residential contract forms, optionally with a real estate attorney's help for $300–600.
What forms does the offer use? +

Texas standard: the TREC One to Four Family Residential Contract (form 20-17). Plus any applicable addenda — the seller's disclosure (already prepared), third-party financing addendum if you're getting a mortgage, etc. All forms are free at trec.texas.gov.

What documentation should I include with my offer? +

Two things make any offer stronger:

  • Pre-approval letter from your lender (for financed offers)
  • Proof of funds — a recent bank statement or account screenshot (for cash offers)

Without one of these, the offer is harder to take seriously. With them, it goes to the top of the pile.

Can I get a home inspection? +

Yes — absolutely encouraged. Buyers in Texas typically include an inspection contingency (the "Option Period," 7–10 days is standard) during which they can inspect the home and back out for any reason. The seller has nothing to hide and supports a thorough inspection.

Will the seller pay closing costs? +

That's part of what's negotiable in the offer. Buyer-asked seller concessions are common in current market conditions — feel free to include them in your offer terms and we'll discuss.
